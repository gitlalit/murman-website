Near-zero emission design and green building certification

For new commercial buildings. We set the building on a path to near-zero emission and climate resilience at design stage, when changes cost least, and carry it through a recognised certification.

Who it is for

Developers and owners of new offices, hospitals, hotels, data centres and institutional buildings. It works best before the design freezes.

What we do

  • Feasibility: which rating is reachable (EDGE, IGBC, GRIHA or LEED), the incremental cost, and what comes back
  • The green FSI case in UDCPR areas: Navi Mumbai, Thane, Kalyan-Dombivli and Pune
  • Passive design review: orientation, shading, glazing and the roof, before any machine is sized
  • Energy and water targets against a baseline building
  • A credit plan that names who does what, and documentation through pre-certification and final certification

What you get

  • A feasibility report with costs and returns
  • A credit plan and responsibility chart
  • A green FSI area statement where it applies
  • The submission set, prepared with accredited professionals
  • A handover file of measured performance

How it is priced

The feasibility check is a defined job. Certification support is a fixed fee that depends on built-up area and the rating chosen. The certifier's own fees are paid at actuals. All of it is quoted on scope.

Twelve components we work with

No single component makes a building green. A certification counts points across all of them.

  1. Rooftop solar: makes power on site
  2. Cool roof and roof insulation: keeps the top floor cool
  3. External shading: stops the sun before the glass
  4. High-performance glazing: lets light in, keeps heat out
  5. Daylight and fresh air: fewer lights, healthier rooms
  6. Treated water reuse: flushing and gardens from the treatment plant
  7. Efficient cooling plant: the largest power saving
  8. Low-carbon concrete and blocks: GGBS or fly ash mixes, AAC walls
  9. LED lights with sensors: on only when needed
  10. Meters and controls: what is measured gets managed
  11. Native trees, permeable paving: shade, and rain soaks in
  12. Rainwater harvesting: stored, or recharged to ground

Illustration. Components follow the IGBC, GRIHA, LEED and EDGE rating categories.

How it runs

  1. Feasibility

    Two weeks on your drawings and BOQ.

  2. Design integration

    Targets and the credit plan agreed with the architect and the services consultants.

  3. Registration and pre-certification

    The rating is registered. Where green FSI applies, this is the stage that unlocks it.

  4. Records during construction

    Purchases, test reports and site photographs collected as work proceeds.

  5. Final certification

    Submission to the independent certifier and close-out.

Questions we are asked

Which rating should we choose?

EDGE is usually the fastest and least costly route. IGBC or GRIHA is the choice where green FSI or a named-rating rule applies. LEED suits buildings aimed at global tenants. The feasibility check answers this for your project.

How much extra does a green building cost?

IGBC officials put the incremental cost today at 1 to 5% of construction cost, down from 18% for India's first rated building in 2003. There is no audited Indian average, so we price it for your building.

Has Murman delivered a certification before?

Not yet, and we say so plainly. Certification work runs with accredited professionals and an independent certifier. What we bring is three decades of knowing what a site can actually build.

Roof cooling for existing buildings

Indian city cores run 2 to 10°C hotter than their outskirts. The roof takes the sun all day, and on an existing building it is the largest surface an owner can change without disturbing a tenant. We survey the roof, repair and waterproof it, and lay a reflective finish in one job.

Why cities run hot

Concrete, asphalt, glass and dark roofs soak up the sun and give the heat back at night. This is the urban heat island effect. For a building owner, cooling the roof is climate adaptation and mitigation in one step: the building stays usable in the heat, and uses less power to do it.

Illustration, not a completed project. A Chennai pilot took a concrete roof from about 42°C to about 30°C with a reflective coating (Lodha, 2025). Telangana's cool roof programme reports rooms 2.1 to 4.3°C cooler. Hyderabad studies found 10 to 19% less air-conditioning power under cool roofs.

How a roof job runs

  1. Survey

    Slab condition, slope, drains and existing waterproofing are checked. The surface temperature is read.

  2. Repair

    Cracks are cut and filled, slopes corrected, drains cleared.

  3. Waterproof

    A new membrane is laid. A cool roof on a leaking slab fails early.

  4. Reflective finish

    Coating, china mosaic or cool tiles, chosen for use and budget.

  5. Measure

    The surface is read again at the same hour, and the result is recorded.

Questions we are asked

It depends on the area and the repairs needed. We give dates after the survey.

Can people walk on it?

China mosaic and cool tiles are walkable finishes. Coatings suit roofs with light foot traffic.

How is it priced?

Per square foot, quoted after a survey, because the repair and waterproofing needs of each roof differ.

Low-carbon materials and circularity

For structures and envelopes. Embodied carbon is decided in the mix design and the BOQ. We reduce it in the order UNEP recommends: avoid, shift, improve.

Who it is for

Developers, structural consultants and contractors on new buildings, and anyone preparing for the recycled-content targets in the 2025 waste rules.

What we do

  • A whole-life carbon baseline: embodied carbon from materials and construction, plus operational carbon from use
  • Concrete mixes with GGBS or fly ash in place of part of the cement
  • AAC blocks and other low-carbon walling
  • A carbon-costed BOQ: every major item with a lower-carbon alternative and its price
  • A recycled-content plan under the Construction and Demolition Waste Management Rules 2025

What you get

  • A baseline report
  • A BOQ with priced alternatives
  • A supplier list with test reports and product declarations
  • A recycled-content plan for the project

How it is priced

The baseline is a defined job. Advisory work depends on the size of the project. All of it is quoted on scope.

How it runs

  1. Baseline

    Carbon is counted from the drawings and the BOQ.

  2. Options

    The largest items are listed with alternatives and prices.

  3. Specification

    Clauses are written for the tender so the choice survives procurement.

  4. Deliveries

    Challans and test certificates are checked as materials arrive.

  5. As-built statement

    The final carbon figure, measured against the baseline.

Questions we are asked

What is embodied carbon?

The carbon released in making a building: cement, steel, glass, blocks, tiles and transport. It is spent before the building opens and cannot be recovered later. UNEP expects it to grow from about 25% to nearly half of building-sector emissions by 2050.

Do low-carbon mixes cost more?

Often they cost less. Published Indian comparisons show GGBS mixes cheaper than plain cement mixes by Rs 6.5 to 125 per cubic metre. Early strength gain is slower, so de-shuttering times are planned with the structural consultant.

What do the waste rules require?

From April 2026, projects of 20,000 sq m and above must use processed construction and demolition waste: 5% in 2026-27, rising to 25% by 2030-31.

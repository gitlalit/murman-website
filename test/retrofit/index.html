What a retrofit changes

Renovating a building emits 50 to 75% less than building new, according to UNEP. Two common building types, before and after. The drawings show measures, not completed projects, and every building needs its own audit before numbers are promised.

An office with a glass facade

  • Shade first. External fins and overhangs stop the sun before it reaches the glass.
  • Then the glass. Solar control film or low-e glass. One maker reports 62% of solar heat rejected by a retrofit film.
  • Then the plant. Tune and right-size the cooling. Two measured Mumbai retrofits, Mahindra Towers and Godrej Bhavan, cut power use by 14% and 12.3%.

A warehouse

  • Roof first. In a Mumbai-region survey a metal roof ran about 18°C hotter than a white tiled roof at the same hour.
  • Daylight and air. Skylight sheets, ridge ventilators and large slow fans cut lighting hours and keep the floor workable.
  • Then solar. A large unshaded roof suits solar panels, after a check of the purlins and sheet.

Sources: Saint-Gobain Solar Gard reference case, 2023 (maker's figure); NRDC and ASCI retrofit case studies; Lodha roof survey, 2025; ASSOCHAM green warehousing note.

Retrofit in seven steps

  1. Energy audit

    Find where the power goes.

  2. Lights and controls

    LED, sensors, timers.

  3. Cooling

    Service and tune first, replace old plant later.

  4. Roof

    Reflective finish with new waterproofing.

  5. Water

    Fix leaks, change fixtures, reuse treated water.

  6. Renewable energy

    Solar on the roof.

  7. Certification and tracking

    A rating for existing buildings, then yearly review.
